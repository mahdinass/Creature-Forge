# R1 search-failure analysis (offline, post-hoc, 2026-10-14)

Script: `scripts/analyze_r1_search.py` → `experiments/phase2a_r1/results/search_analysis.json`. It reads the committed replicate-1 histories and validations. **No MuJoCo rollouts, no ledger writes.** Everything below is descriptive and post-hoc; it changes nothing in the registered R1 result (gate STOP).

## Question
Did broad uniform sampling in the 13/15-D space produce too few viable gaits for the robustness objectives to have a useful effect?

## Answer: yes, and the lower-tail objective was additionally misaligned with walking

1. **The effective sample was 149 points, not 447.** Common random numbers made arms A and B evaluate identical vectors (verified: bit-equal) and arm C reuse the same first 13 coordinates. The R1 report's "447 non-incumbent candidates" counts evaluations; the number of distinct 13-D points was 149 (C differs only in 2 extra coordinates). Arm comparisons A/B/C therefore compare objectives and 2 extra dimensions on the same draws, which was intended, but it means the run was one 149-point random search.
2. **Uniform sampling never came near the incumbent.** In normalized coordinates the nearest sample was 0.78 (L2) from the incumbent, against a median of 1.41 and a uniform 1st percentile of 0.86; the closest in any single worst coordinate was 0.40. The probability that a uniform draw falls within ±0.3 of the incumbent in every one of the 13 coordinates is 2.1e-4 (expected 0.03 hits in 149; observed 0); within ±0.1 it is 5e-10. The incumbent also sits near bounds in several coordinates (hip_amp at 2% of its range, stance_knee 89%, hip_bias 86%, sync_gain 84%), a small corner of the box.
3. **Almost nothing in the box is a gait.** Of the 149 points × 8 training seeds: **0** seed-level walking outcomes (the 4 "walking" labels in the raw label counts belong to the incumbent, candidate 0); 1,092/1,192 seed outcomes are plain falls (median fall time 1.26 s), 68 falling-forward, 18 body-dragging, 10 contact-chatter, 2 hops, 2 backward shuffles. 78 of 149 points produced at least one valid step on some seed, only 8 produced ≥3 (maximum 8; the incumbent makes 25-34 on its walking seeds). 14 points survived at least one seed; only 2 survived ≥4/8; none survived all 8. Arm C (with start_lean/ramp) looked similar (61 with a valid step, 10 with ≥3).
4. **The mean objective could never select a candidate over the incumbent.** Walking seeds score ≈ 5.99, non-walking ones ≈ −0.4 to −0.8; the incumbent's training mean is 2.63 (4/8 walking), the best random point 0.62. Arm A therefore returned the incumbent as its rank-1 and its other four picks (A1 indices 11, 62, 80, 129) did not walk on any validation seed.
5. **The worst-2 objective ranked the walking incumbent 140th of 150.** Its two lowest seeds (fitness −0.83 and −0.75; backward-drifting falls) are worse than a typical random fall (median per-seed fitness −0.41), so 139 of 149 random points had a *better* worst-2 score than the incumbent although none walked. The lower-tail objective as registered rewards *not drifting backward while falling or standing*, not walking. Arms B and C consequently picked survivors with no valid walking (C's best validated survived 16/16 seeds with 0 walking). This is a defect of the objective's interaction with the fitness terms (progress is signed and multiplied by 3), not of robustness training as an idea.
6. **Evaluation was not the weak link.** Training and validation agreed closely on the 14 validated candidates (Spearman 0.98 on mean fitness, dominated by many zeros, so weak evidence), and training survival fractions tracked validation survival (e.g. 0.75 → 0.875, 0.625 → 0.688, 0 → 0). Noise across seeds did not hide a good candidate; there was none.
7. **Which parameters matter is unresolved by this data.** Spearman correlations of normalized parameters with mean fitness/survival over the 149 draws are weak (|ρ| ≤ 0.35; the largest is hip_amp vs survival count, +0.35: smaller hip amplitude survived longer; kd vs mean fitness −0.25). These are uncorrected, post-hoc and based on ≈ no viable samples; they are not evidence that the other parameters are unimportant. The Phase 2A ablation (pitch feedback and ankle balance essential) is unaffected.

## Consequences for design
- A global random search in this box needs a different tool (not available at this budget), so R2 should be explicitly **local**: it can only answer *preservation and refinement*, not *discovery*.
- Do not use a worst-k mean of the continuous fitness. Use the count of seeds that pass every walking gate (a discrete, comparable success measure) as the primary training criterion, with a preservation constraint, then fitness as a tie-breaker.
- 30 final seeds is too few: the exact two-sided McNemar test on 30 paired seeds has only ≈ 23% power for a +15 pp improvement (+10 pp: 9%). R2 proposes 150 (power 96% / 74% for +15 / +10 pp under the assumed discordance patterns).

## Not claimed
No claim that robustness training cannot work; no causal statement about which parameter drives startup success; no new rollouts were run.
