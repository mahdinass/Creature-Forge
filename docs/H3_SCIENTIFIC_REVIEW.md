# H3 focused scientific review — 26 October 2026

This is a targeted primary-source comparison, not a systematic review or novelty search. Abstract-only findings are distinguished from full-text findings. Existing negative Creature Forge studies motivate better identification; they do not provide positive evidence for a new learner.

| Primary source consulted | Closest mechanism and implication for Creature Forge |
|---|---|
| Yin, Loken & van de Panne, **SIMBICON** (2007), [author full text](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf), §§3–4 | Contact/time-triggered pose control, torso and swing-leg feedback, and feedback-error learning. This is close to existing PD/phase/FP control. Adding contact gates or foot placement alone would repeat established and already implemented ideas. Small feedforward torque terms are likewise not a novel control principle. |
| Cully et al., **Robots that can adapt like animals** (Nature 2015), [author arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3501), abstract | Precompute a repertoire of useful behaviors, then adapt after damage with intelligent trial and error. A meaningful Creature Forge comparison must account for repertoire-generation cost and compare with conventional search; sparse failed histories do not supply that repertoire. The Nature page fetch redirected unsuccessfully; this account uses the accessible author version. |
| Bongard, Zykov & Lipson, **Resilient machines through continuous self-modeling** (Science 2006), [original article abstract indexed by PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17110570/), DOI10.1126/science.1133687 | Infer structure through actuation/sensation and use the updated model to construct alternative locomotion. Relevant to diagnosis-to-action closure, not just detecting model mismatch. We consulted the authors' abstract, not inaccessible full-text details; EM1 already warns that mismatch need not change the best action. |
| Kumar et al., **RMA** (RSS2021), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034), abstract | Base policy plus adaptation module, trained in simulation for varied deployment conditions. RMA demonstrates an established adaptation architecture, not a low-cost guarantee for our 18 historical bases. A comparable project would need a broad training population and matched policy baselines. |
| Kumar et al., **A-RMA** (2022), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15299), abstract | Biped extension adapts the base policy to an imperfect inferred extrinsics vector. This addresses a relevant failure of plug-in diagnosis: even a useful latent estimate may harm a policy trained for perfect extrinsics. It weakens the case for bolting a diagnostic neural network onto the existing controller without joint validation. |
| Johannink et al., **Residual Reinforcement Learning for Robot Control** (2019), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03201), abstract | Combine conventional control with a learned residual; demonstrated on contact-rich manipulation. Our two-gain residual would be conventional parameter search, **not residual RL**, and planar damage locomotion is a different setting. Only controlled physical benefit in this setting could be claimed. |
| Chua et al., **PETS** (NeurIPS2018), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12114), abstract | Probabilistic ensembles and trajectory sampling represent dynamics uncertainty in model-based RL. Useful alternative when a model is actionable; requires additional data and planning cost. Better prediction/Brier alone is insufficient: repair utility must improve beyond EM1 and physical-only baselines. |
| Ahn et al., **SayCan** (2022), [authors' project and paper link](https://say-can.github.io/) | Ground language-model planning using available skills and their value/affordance functions. It cannot create missing low-level capability by ranking unavailable repairs. A Creature Forge learned selector would need verified skills and predecision applicability targets; H1 does not yet provide adequate independent support. |

These sources cover damage recovery, feedback/adaptive locomotion, morphology/condition adaptation, reusable skills, model-based learning and capability-grounded planning. No generic guarantee for traction failure, saturated weak actuators or unseen morphologies follows. No medical/hardware safety claim is involved.

The closest modest project contribution is an **identified controller-family ablation**: preserve the current FP/contact controller and actuator limits, add a nested asymmetric stance basis, and compare with equal and double search budgets using new lineages and repeated physical verification. The possible research value is separating structural from search limitations under unreliable verification. A positive pilot would justify a larger registered study; a negative pilot would constrain this specific basis. Neither outcome would establish neural learning or Nemotron value.

For later AI research, first demonstrate that multiple controller families solve different, repeatable subsets of independent robots. Only then freeze predecision observations and test a selector against best fixed family, equal-budget physical search and random selection. History claims additionally require sham/shuffled histories and lineage-held-out evaluation. Offline training and repertoire construction must be charged to total compute.
